# Optional EP and album planning

Use this mode for collection-level requests or relevant cross-track context, not as a prerequisite for a song.

Establish the premise and listening experience: narrative, thematic, atmospheric, or a collection of distinct songs. Require no shared narrator or resolved ending. Define a useful palette of instruments, vocal identity, motifs, lyrical world, or production character.

Give each track a job. A compact track map can show working title, angle, vocal/instrumental status, energy, groove, and relation to adjacent tracks. Leave undecided titles and lengths provisional.

Sequence for perceived movement. Tempo alone is not energy; density, rhythm, register, and emotional pressure also matter. Consider the opener, transitions, and what the closer leaves behind. Prescribe no strongest-track positions, fixed track count, or mandatory tempo alternation.

Use callbacks when they acquire meaning. Preserve each song's intended identity; do not insert a reference merely to fill a motif table. For single-track revision, read only sibling material needed for context and edit only the authorized scope. Preserve deliberate changes of voice, language, tone, and genre.

For real people/events, distinguish supplied facts from invention and verify claims when accuracy matters. Do not impose a documentary research pipeline on fictional songwriting.

Return the requested concept and track map. Artwork, mixing/mastering, promotion, distribution, uploads, and release management remain out of scope.
