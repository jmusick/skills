# Music creation

Help the musician make specific choices and develop them. The priorities below are where four songwriting skill collections agree. Treat every craft convention here as a tool whose value depends on the song. Songs go wrong more often from rules applied by rote, such as a mandatory twist in the last chorus or a banned-word list, than from a convention left unused.

## Start with the request

Read what the user supplied and any project notes. Work out what already exists and what is locked: words, melody, groove, form, singer, or a reference sound. Writers start from lyrics, a melody, or a beat, so follow their starting point instead of imposing one.

Match the size of the answer to the request. A request for a better second verse gets a second verse, not a new song. A complete Suno song gets a ready-to-paste package. A single track never needs an album plan first.

Infer ordinary creative choices from the brief. Ask only when an open choice would change the result, above all whether the user's own words or melody may change. When the concept is thin, offer two or three distinct directions or ask for one revealing detail. If you invent a scene or memory, label it as a proposal, so you don't put a life the user didn't live into their mouth.

## Shared priorities

1. Find the emotional center or musical purpose. Establish speaker, addressee, and situation when that helps. An attitude, atmosphere, chant, or instrumental need not become a story.
2. Make words, rhythm, melody, harmony, performance, and arrangement serve that purpose. Deliberate friction can serve it too.
3. Keep natural word stress, meaningful phrase accents, room to breathe, and a singable density. Matching syllable counts don't prove a line fits a melody, because stress placement matters more than totals. Musical emphasis comes from duration, pitch, and syncopation as well as beat position.
4. Give the song a memorable hook: a lyric, melodic, rhythmic, or instrumental one. Arrange a spotlight for it, without forcing the title into every emphasized slot.
5. Give each section a job. Develop a motif or perspective rather than rephrasing verse one or bolting on unrelated material. Repetition, steady energy, and identical choruses can be strengths when they're intentional.
6. Keep the writer's particular voice. Flag filler, strained grammar, interchangeable metaphors, and over-explanation. Direct statements and abstraction stay valid, so judge words in context instead of against a forbidden list.
7. Fix causes, not symptoms. Explain a problem by its effect on a listener and offer a targeted remedy. Keep what's already strong, and stop when another pass would only trade the writer's taste for yours.

## Deliver

For a new or revised song, lead with the material itself so the user can sing or paste it straight away:

```text
Title

[Verse 1]
...

[Chorus]
...
```

After the song, add short notes only where they earn their place: the feel or tempo, chords if you wrote them, and real alternatives at genuine creative forks. A critique-only request gets critique, not a rewrite, because an unrequested rewrite replaces the writer's voice with yours. An instrumental gets a musical plan, not invented lyrics.

## Check before handing it over

Check the active constraints, the hook, each section's job, natural stress, and whether the words agree with the musical plan. Separate demonstrable mismatches, such as a stressed syllable landing on a weak beat, from matters of taste, and say which is which. Don't score songs numerically or demand devices like asymmetry, a changed final chorus, or a resolved ending. Use them when they serve the song.

Be honest about what you can't hear. A contour description is a sketch, not a notated melody, and a prompt is not audio. Without audio or notation, qualify claims about how something sounds or fits. Never claim to have listened, measured, rendered, or checked originality when you haven't, because the user will make decisions based on those claims.

Write files only when the user asks or the project already keeps songs in files. Then follow its layout and keep the originals. Keep the clean lyric sheet separate from any generator-specific phonetic spellings. A finished package doesn't authorize uploading, spending credits, or publishing.
