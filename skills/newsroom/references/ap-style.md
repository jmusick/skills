# AP style reference

House style (`house-style.md`) overrides anything here. When in doubt about an entry not listed, say so and suggest checking the current AP Stylebook rather than guessing.

## Numbers

- Spell out one through nine. Use figures for 10 and up.
- Always use figures for ages, percentages, money, addresses, times, dates, dimensions, temperatures, scores, votes, and political districts and wards: `a 6-year-old girl`, `5%`, `$4`, `5 cents`, `123 Main St.`, `voted 5-2`, `3rd Ward`.
- Spell out any number that starts a sentence. The exception is a year: `1998 was ...`. Rewrite the sentence if that reads badly.
- Write large round numbers as `$1.2 million` or `3 billion`, not `$1,200,000`. Use commas in figures of 1,000 and up, but not in years or street addresses.
- Ordinals: spell out first through ninth for order and place (`first place`, `ninth grade`). Use figures from 10th up.
- Percent: write `%` with the figure (`5%`, `0.5%`). Use "percentage points" for the difference between two percentages. A rise from 4% to 6% is 2 percentage points.
- Ages: `Jane Smith, 42,` and `a 42-year-old woman`, but `the woman is 42`.

## Dates and times

- With a specific date, abbreviate Jan., Feb., Aug., Sept., Oct., Nov., Dec. Never abbreviate March, April, May, June, or July. Spell out every month when it stands alone or appears with only a year: `October 2026`.
- No ordinals on dates: write `Oct. 14`, not `Oct. 14th`. Put a comma after the year when it falls mid-sentence: `Oct. 14, 2026, at ...`.
- Within seven days of publication, use the day of the week (`Tuesday`). Beyond that, use the date. Don't write "on Tuesday" unless leaving out "on" would confuse.
- Write times as `7 p.m.` and `7:30 a.m.`, not `7:00 PM`. Use `noon` and `midnight`, never `12 p.m.`. Don't stack redundancies like `10 a.m. this morning`.
- Order is time, day, date, place: `7 p.m. Tuesday, Oct. 14, at City Hall`.
- Ranges: `9 a.m. to 5 p.m.`, `8-11 a.m.`, `from 2019 to 2024`, or `2019-24`. Never mix forms, as in `from 2019-2024`.
- Decades: `the 1990s`, `the '90s`.

## Titles and names

- Capitalize formal titles directly before a name: `Mayor Jane Smith`, `Superintendent John Doe`, `Police Chief Ann Lee`. Lowercase them after a name or on their own: `Smith, the mayor`, `the superintendent`.
- Occupational descriptions stay lowercase: `attorney Jane Smith`, `teacher John Doe`.
- Put long titles after the name: `Jane Smith, director of the county board of elections`.
- Abbreviate these before a full name: `Gov.`, `Lt. Gov.`, `Rep.`, `Sen.`, `Dr.`. Use `Dr.` only for physicians, dentists, and similar practitioners.
- Party and hometown take this form: `state Rep. Jane Smith, R-Hometown`, `U.S. Sen. John Doe, D-Ohio`.

## Places

- Spell out state names in body text. **Never abbreviate Ohio.** AP abbreviations (`Mich.`, `Pa.`, `Ind.`) are only for datelines, lists, and party labels. Postal codes (`OH`) are only for full mailing addresses with a ZIP code.
- Cities on AP's dateline list stand alone, among them Cleveland and Cincinnati. Other cities take the state: `Toledo, Ohio`.
- Addresses: abbreviate `St.`, `Ave.`, and `Blvd.` only with a numbered address (`123 Main St.`, but `on Main Street`). Never abbreviate Road, Drive, or Lane. Write `123 W. Main St.`.

## Word choice

| Use | Not |
| --- | --- |
| that (restrictive, no commas) / which (nonrestrictive, with commas) | interchangeable |
| who for people | that for people |
| fewer for things you count, less for amounts | less people |
| more than, or over (AP accepts both with numbers) | in excess of |
| about | approximately |
| because | due to the fact that |
| to | in order to |
| use | utilize |
| crash or collision | accident (implies no one is at fault) |
| pleaded | pled |
| compared with (differences) / compared to (likeness) | interchangeable |
| affect (verb) / effect (noun) | the reverse |
| website, email, internet, online | Web site, e-mail |
| anti-abortion / abortion-rights | pro-life / pro-choice |

## Punctuation

- No Oxford comma in a simple series unless house style says otherwise: `red, white and blue`.
- Periods and commas go inside quotation marks. Colons and semicolons go outside. Question marks go inside only when they're part of the quote.
- Use single quotation marks in headlines and for a quote within a quote.
- Hyphenate a compound modifier before a noun (`full-time job`) but never after an -ly adverb (`highly regarded teacher`).
- AP puts a space on both sides of a dash. Keep dashes rare; see `ai-tells.md`.
- Spell out an abbreviation on first reference unless readers know it on sight (FBI, NASA). On second reference, prefer a plain noun ("the board," "the district") to an acronym.

## Sensitive subjects

- Mention race, disability, gender identity, immigration status, or sexual orientation only when it's relevant to the story. Capitalize `Black` and `Indigenous`, and lowercase `white`.
- Put the person first: `a person with an addiction`, not `an addict`. Write `homeless people` or `people experiencing homelessness`, not `the homeless`.
- Use the age in place of `elderly` or `senior citizen`.
- Suicide: write `died by suicide` or `killed himself`, never `committed suicide`. Don't describe the method in detail. Include the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline.
- Sexual assault: don't name victims without their consent.
