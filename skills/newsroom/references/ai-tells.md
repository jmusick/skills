# AI tells and local-news cliches

These patterns make copy read as machine-written or lazily written. Readers who spot them start doubting the facts around them. Fix each one by going back to the facts: who did what, how many, and when. Swapping in a synonym just makes a new tell.

## Cut on sight

- **Staged contrasts:** "It's not X, it's Y." / "Not just X, but Y." State what is true.
- **Set-up questions:** "The result?" / "Why does it matter?" / "The catch?" Give the answer.
- **Throat-clearing:** "Here's the thing," "It's worth noting," "Notably," "Interestingly," "Importantly." Delete them.
- **Significance inflation:** "marks a pivotal moment," "a watershed," "historic" (unless it's literally a first), "sends a strong message," "underscores." State what happened.
- **-ing tails:** "..., highlighting the community's commitment to ..." End the sentence at the fact.
- **Vague sources:** "experts say," "many believe," "critics argue," "some residents." Name them or cut the claim.
- **Empty closers:** "Only time will tell," "It remains to be seen," "The future looks bright," any closing paragraph that sums up the story or draws a moral.
- **AI vocabulary:** delve, landscape, tapestry, testament, robust, vibrant, seamless, leverage, foster, navigate (when nobody is driving), multifaceted, game-changer.

## Local-news cliches

- bustling, nestled, quaint, close-knit or tight-knit community, rich history, hidden gem, beloved, staple
- "came together," "rallied around," "packed house" (give the count), "standing room only" (unless people were literally standing)
- "heated debate," "hot-button issue," "slammed," "blasted," "sparked outrage" (describe what people said and did)
- "eyesore," "a sign of the times," "only in a small town," "it's no secret"

## Limits

- At most one dash per 1,000 words. Use a period, comma, or colon instead.
- Avoid reflexive lists of three, and use one only when there really are three things.
- Repeat the right noun ("the board ... the board") instead of rotating synonyms ("the board ... the panel ... the body").
- Vary sentence and paragraph length. A run of paragraphs that all look the same reads as generated.
- Don't stack hedges ("could potentially," "may possibly"). Hedge once, only as far as the source does, and don't hedge confirmed facts.
