# Signals: what to look for, and which service fixes it

Check a candidate in about five minutes: the website, the Google Business Profile (from a Google or Maps search for the name and town), and the Facebook Page. Record what you found and where.

Rank candidates by how much the problem likely costs them in customers. A missing website loses a restaurant more than an old copyright year does.

| Signal | How to check | Service to pitch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No website, or only a Facebook Page | No site on Google, Maps, or Facebook | Design & Development: a website |
| Website domain is dead, expired, or parked | Open the URL; look for registrar "for sale" or hosting error pages | Hosting & Security, then a website |
| No HTTPS, or a browser security warning | Load `https://`; check for a certificate error or "Not secure" | Hosting & Security: SSL and hosting |
| Breaks on a phone | Resize the browser to about 390 px wide: text you can't read, sideways scrolling, a menu that won't open | Design & Development: redesign |
| Very slow | The page takes more than a few seconds to show content | Hosting & Security: performance |
| Clearly unmaintained | Copyright years ago, events or menus from past seasons, "coming soon" pages, broken links or images | Design & Development, or a maintenance plan |
| No way to act online where customers would expect one | A restaurant with no menu or ordering, a salon or contractor with no booking or quote form, a shop with no store | Design & Development: booking, ordering, eCommerce |
| Contact info is hard to find, or differs between site, Google, and Facebook | Compare hours, phone, and address across the three | Marketing: Google Business Profile and local SEO |
| Missing or thin Google Business Profile | No hours, no photos, no website link, unanswered questions | Marketing: local SEO |
| Google profile is unclaimed, or its Website button leads to a dead or unrelated page | "Claim this business" shows on the Maps listing; click the Website button and see where it lands | Marketing: Google Business Profile and local SEO |
| Doesn't come up when you search for its own category and town | Search "bakery Huron Ohio" and see whether it appears | Marketing: SEO |
| Facebook Page gone quiet (no posts in months) | The Page's latest post date | Marketing: social media |
| Mismatched or low-quality logo and branding | A pixelated logo, a different logo on each platform | Branding & Design |
| Old platform the owner can't update | Footer says an outdated builder, or the site uses Flash or frames | Design & Development: migration |

## What isn't a signal

- A simple site that works. Plain isn't broken, and "you could look more modern" is a weak pitch.
- Anything you couldn't confirm, such as a guess at their traffic or sales.
- Reviews about the business itself (food, service). That's not SDM's work, and mentioning it is rude.

## Making the observation land

Name the problem in the owner's terms: what a customer sees or can't do. "Someone searching on their phone for your hours gets an error page" works. "Your site lacks a responsive viewport meta tag" doesn't. One clear, checked observation beats three.
